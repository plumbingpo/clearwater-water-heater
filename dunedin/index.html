How Dunedin Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing discolored water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Dunedin

Long before a tank floods a Dunedin garage, it almost always sends signals. Look out for rumbling and popping while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a failed tank and water on the floor. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it needs a professional look soon. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
