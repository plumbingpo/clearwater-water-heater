Clear Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Clearwater Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing on every job, covering everything from a failed heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

The trouble with water heater failures is that many of them develop inside the tank where you can't see them. Hard-water minerals build a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod gets eaten away gradually, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. If the water turns rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap with water on the floor. Our technicians know how hard water and local conditions wear on tanks over time, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Clearwater Homes

Each maintenance visit from Clearwater Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and sets a safe water temperature. Minor faults get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and the heater keeps running clean and efficient.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. An older tank gets a closer look than a new heater, a tankless unit on hard water calls for regular descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a large family cycles the tank far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your equipment and water, so every tune-up keeps the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already paid for. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve in working order, which lowers monthly energy bills while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most families, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Right-Sized Hot Water

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Getting the size right is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before quoting a new heater, Clearwater Water Heater works through the details that drive your household's peak demand: how many people and bathrooms you have, back-to-back showers, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and your incoming water temperature. The result is a heater with enough first-hour capacity or GPM to cover your busiest hour without wasting energy on excess capacity.

Picking a new water heater comes down to more than the number of gallons. Our technician will compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, while a properly sized tank is often the simpler, less expensive choice. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Homes in Clearwater run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Clearwater Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with an expansion tank plus a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before they spread. We also verify the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
