Keeping Your Largo Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Largo

Whatever type of water heater your Largo home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric tanks burn out heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
