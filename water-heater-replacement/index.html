Why Professional Water Heater Replacement Matters

Consider the vent first. A gas water heater's exhaust contains carbon monoxide, and a vent that is undersized, poorly pitched, or loosely connected can let it spill back into your home. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and the details pile up quickly. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code, and some jurisdictions require a permit. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
